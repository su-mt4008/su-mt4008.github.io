## The question

**Has Stockholm got warmer since the late 1990s?**

## The data

Daily mean air temperature at SMHI’s weather station **Stockholm-Observatoriekullen**, from
October 1996 to June 2026: [smhi-stockholm-daily-temperature.csv](/data/hw1/smhi-stockholm-daily-temperature.csv)
(626 KB). Source: SMHI Open Data, station 98230, parameter 2. Licence: CC BY 4.0
(credit SMHI). Retrieved 2026-11-03.

Download the file and save it **unchanged** in `data/raw/`. Open it in a text editor first: the
first nine lines describe the station, and the data starts on line 10, with columns separated
by semicolons and Swedish column names. Read it like this:

:::code-group
```python [Python]
import pandas as pd

raw = pd.read_csv(
    "data/raw/smhi-stockholm-daily-temperature.csv",
    sep=";", skiprows=9, header=0,
    usecols=[2, 3], names=["date", "temp"], parse_dates=["date"],
)
```
```r [R]
raw <- read.csv("data/raw/smhi-stockholm-daily-temperature.csv", sep = ";", skip = 9)[, 3:4]
names(raw) <- c("date", "temp")
raw$date <- as.Date(raw$date)
```
:::

`date` is the day and `temp` the mean temperature that day, in °C.

## Tasks

Follow [How we work](/guide#hand-in-an-assignment): clone `<username>-hw1`, run `uv sync` or
`renv::restore()`, and make a branch called `hw1`. Then:

0. **Remove the example.** Delete `data/raw/penguins.csv`, replace its row in
   `data/raw/README.md` with the SMHI file (source, licence, retrieval date), and replace the
   penguin text and code in `report.qmd` with your own. Put your name in the header.

Then write `report.qmd`, one section at a time:

1. **Question** — state the question in your own words, and say in one or two sentences why
   someone might care about the answer.
2. **Data** — where the data comes from and what it measures. Then: what can this data
   *not* tell you? (Think: one station, in a city, over 30 years.)
3. **Cleaning** — read the file with the code above. Some years are incomplete: count the
   days per year, decide which years to use, and say why. Do everything in code; never edit
   the file.
4. **Exploration** — one figure of the mean temperature per year, with labelled axes and a
   caption. Describe what you see in two or three sentences.
5. **Analysis** — compare the mean annual temperature of **1997–2010** with **2011–2025**: a
   small table with the number of years, the mean and the standard deviation for each period.
6. **Conclusions & limitations** — answer the question in two to four sentences. Name at least
   one limitation, and say whether the difference could be chance given how much single years
   vary. (We learn how to test that later in the course; here, an honest sentence is enough.)
7. **How to reproduce** — the commands that render your report.

Render with `uv run quarto render report.qmd` (Python) or `quarto render report.qmd` (R), read
`report.md`, and commit `report.qmd`, `report.md`, `report_files/` and the data.

## Peer review

After the deadline you get access to a classmate’s HW1 repository. Review their pull request by
the peer-review deadline, as described in [Review a pull request](/guide#review-a-pull-request).
For HW1, check especially:

- Are all seven sections there, in order?
- Is the check green? Does the cleaning say which years were used, and why?
- Do the conclusions follow from the table and the figure, without claiming more?
- One highlight and one concrete suggestion.

## Scoring

HW1 is scored 0–100, and you need 65 to pass. Late hand-ins and the final deadline: see
[Assignments](/homework/). We look for:

- all seven sections, each doing its job;
- a green check (the report renders from a fresh clone);
- cleaning decisions written down and done in code;
- a clear figure and table, and conclusions that the evidence supports;
- a constructive peer review.
