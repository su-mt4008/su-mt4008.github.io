# Homework 4 — Joins and SQL

## Overview

In HW4 you combine data from two organisations to answer one question. The focus is the week 4
material: **keys** that match, **joins** that neither lose nor duplicate rows, and **SQL**. The
statistics is a correlation and a slope, and a careful look at what else could explain them.

The question is given; how you measure “faster” is your choice. Write down every choice and why.
A reviewer should be able to check each join by its row counts.

Read [week 4](/lectures/week-4) first. It works through the same steps on Swedish municipalities.

## Learning goals

- Check that a key is unique, and make keys from different sources match.

## The question

**Did mobile phones spread faster in poorer countries?**

In 1995, mobile phones were rare almost everywhere. Twenty years later, most countries had more
mobile subscriptions than people. A common story is that poorer countries **leapfrogged**: they
skipped landlines and went straight to mobile phones, and adopted them faster. Is that what the
data show?

You decide how to measure “faster” and “poorer”. One possibility: the number of years a country
took to go from 10 to 50 mobile subscriptions per 100 people, against its GDP per capita in the
year it reached 10. Other measures are fine if you say why you chose them.

## The data

Four files. The first is from Gapminder, the other three from the World Bank’s World Development
Indicators. All four were retrieved on 2026-11-06 and are licensed
[CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/): you may use them freely, but you must
credit the source.

| File | What it is | Source |
|---|---|---|
| [gapminder-cell-phones-total.csv](/data/hw4/gapminder-cell-phones-total.csv) (151 KB) | mobile cellular subscriptions per country and year, 1960–2024 | Gapminder, [Systema Globalis](https://github.com/open-numbers/ddf--gapminder--systema_globalis), indicator `cell_phones_total` (based on ITU data, via the World Bank) |
| [worldbank-population.csv](/data/hw4/worldbank-population.csv) (197 KB) | population per country and year, 1960–2025 | World Bank, [SP.POP.TOTL](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.POP.TOTL) |
| [worldbank-gdp-per-capita.csv](/data/hw4/worldbank-gdp-per-capita.csv) (217 KB) | GDP per capita, PPP, in constant 2021 international dollars, 1990–2025 | World Bank, [NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD) |
| [worldbank-countries.csv](/data/hw4/worldbank-countries.csv) (65 KB) | region and income group of each country | World Bank, the country list that comes with the indicators above |

Things to know before you start:

- **Subscriptions are not people.** One person can have several SIM cards, so a country can have
  more than 100 subscriptions per 100 people. Say what this means for your answer.
- **GDP per capita, PPP** is the value of everything a country produces in a year, divided by its
  population, and adjusted for price levels so that a dollar buys the same in every country. It
  is a common measure of how rich a country is on average. These data start in 1990.
- **The World Bank files** have four lines of information before the header, one column per year,
  and an empty last column. They contain countries and also **groups of countries** (“World”,
  “High income”, “Sub-Saharan Africa”, …). In `worldbank-countries.csv`, the groups have no
  `Region`.
- **The income group** in `worldbank-countries.csv` is the World Bank’s *current* classification,
  not the one in the year a country got its phones.
- **The two sources use different codes.** Gapminder writes `swe`, the World Bank `SWE`, and
  a few countries have different codes altogether.

Save the four files **unchanged** in `hw4/data/raw/`, and fill in their rows in
`hw4/data/raw/README.md`. They read like this:


## Tasks

Follow [How we work](/guide#hand-in-an-assignment): update `main`, and make a branch called
`hw4` from it. Then write `hw4/report.qmd`, one section at a time:

0. **Set up the folder.** Your name in the header of `hw4/report.qmd`, and a row per file in
   `hw4/data/raw/README.md` (source, licence, retrieval date).
1. **Question** — state the question in your own words, with **your** measure of “faster” and of
   “poorer”, and say why someone might care about the answer.
2. **Data** — what one row of each file is, where it comes from, and the years it covers. What
   can these data *not* tell you? (Think: subscriptions against users, countries against people,
   today’s income groups.)
3. **Cleaning** — read the files and show that they were read correctly. Then:
   - reshape the two World Bank tables to one row per country and year
     ([week 3](/lectures/week-3));
   - make the country codes match, check that the keys are unique, and find the codes that don’t
     match, **in both directions**, with anti joins. Decide what to do with each, and say why;
   - leave out the groups of countries;
   - join phones with population, compute **subscriptions per 100 people**, and then join what
     else you need;
   - show a small table with the **number of rows before and after every join**, and explain
     every change.
4. **Exploration** — at least two figures, each with a caption saying what to see:
   - subscriptions per 100 people over time for a handful of countries of your choice, from
     different income groups;
   - one more figure of your choice that helps with the question, for example the year each
     country reached your first threshold against its GDP per capita.
5. **Analysis** — answer the question:
   - compute your measure of speed for each country. Some series start above your first
     threshold, have gaps, or never reach the second: decide which countries you can use, say
     how many, and why;
   - a scatter plot of speed against GDP per capita (consider a log scale), with the correlation
     and the least-squares slope, in units a reader understands
     ([statistics section 6](/statistics#_6-relationships-correlation-and-trend));
   - a table by income group: number of countries, median and mean speed;
   - **in SQL**: put your tidy phones, population and countries tables into an SQLite database
     in memory (`sqlite3.connect(":memory:")` and `to_sql()`, or `dbConnect(RSQLite::SQLite(),
     ":memory:")` and `dbWriteTable()`), and write queries that give
     1. the ten countries with the fewest subscriptions per 100 people in 2010
        (`JOIN`, `WHERE`, `ORDER BY`, `LIMIT`);
     2. for each income group, the number of countries and the mean subscriptions per 100 people
        in 2010 (`GROUP BY`);
     3. the number of rows of your phones–population join for 2010, which must equal the number
        you get in pandas or dplyr.

     Show the SQL text in the report, and a few rows of each result.
6. **Conclusions & limitations** — answer the question in a few sentences, with numbers. Then
   think about **when** the countries started: did poorer and richer countries reach your first
   threshold in the same years? If not, how could that affect a comparison of speeds
   ([statistics section 7](/statistics#_7-correlation-is-not-causation))? Name at least one more
   limitation.
7. **How to reproduce** — the commands that render your report.

Every number in your text should come from the code (inline code, see
[week 2](/lectures/week-2#tables-and-numbers-in-a-report)). Every figure follows the
[checklist for honest plots](/lectures/week-2#honest-plots).

Render with `uv run quarto render hw4/report.qmd` (Python) or `quarto render hw4/report.qmd`
(R), read `hw4/report.md`, and commit everything in `hw4/`. Change nothing outside `hw4/`, except
the lockfile if you added a package.
