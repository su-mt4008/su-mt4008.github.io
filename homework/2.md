# Homework 2 — Tables and plots

## Overview

In HW2 you go through the whole cycle again, on a dataset with more to decide. The focus is
the week 2 material: **reading** a file correctly, **shaping** it with the five verbs,
**summarising groups** with their uncertainty, and **plots that tell the truth**.

This time the question is given, but more of the choices are yours: how to group the
apartments, what to leave out, which plots answer the question. Write down every choice and why.
A reviewer should be able to disagree with a decision and still follow your report.

Read [week 2](/lectures/week-2) first. It works through the same steps on another dataset.

## Learning goals

- Read a real file and check that it was read correctly.
- Derive a variable, group the data, and summarise each group with its size, centre, spread and
  a 95% confidence interval.
- Choose plots that fit the question, and draw them honestly.
- Write conclusions that state their uncertainty and their limits.
- Give a specific, helpful review.

## The question

**How does the price per square metre depend on the number of rooms, for apartments sold in
Ekhagen?**

Ekhagen is a neighbourhood in northern Stockholm, next to the university. A common belief is
that small apartments cost more per square metre. Is that true here, and by how much?

## The data

Sales of 158 apartments in Ekhagen between October 2012 and November 2018, collected from the
property site Booli in 2018 for this course:
[booli-ekhagen-sold.csv](/data/hw2/booli-ekhagen-sold.csv) (41 KB).
Each row is one sale. The columns you need:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `soldPrice` | the final price, in SEK |
| `listPrice` | the asking price when it was advertised, in SEK |
| `livingArea` | living area, in m² |
| `rooms` | number of rooms; half rooms (1.5, 2.5) are small extra rooms |
| `soldDate` | the date of the sale |
| `rent` | the monthly fee to the housing association, in SEK |
| `constructionYear`, `floor` | when the building was built, and the floor |

The other columns (address, coordinates, the broker) you can leave out; say that you did.

Save the file **unchanged** in `hw2/data/raw/`, and fill in its row in `hw2/data/raw/README.md`.
Open it in a text editor first, and choose your reading arguments from what you see.

## Tasks

Follow [How we work](/guide#hand-in-an-assignment): update `main`, and make a branch called
`hw2` from it. Then write `hw2/report.qmd`, one section at a time:

0. **Set up the folder.** Your name in the header of `hw2/report.qmd`, and the data’s row in
   `hw2/data/raw/README.md` (source, licence or terms, retrieval date).
1. **Question** — state the question in your own words, and say why a buyer or a seller might care.
2. **Data** — what one row is, the period, and how the data were collected. What can it *not*
   tell you? (Think: one neighbourhood, only apartments that were sold, the years covered.)
3. **Cleaning** — read the file and show that it was read correctly (shape, types). Then:
   - derive the price per m², `soldPrice / livingArea`;
   - handle missing values: count them per column you use, and drop only the rows you have to;
   - decide how to group the number of rooms. There are few apartments with 4.5, 5 or 6 rooms,
     and some with half rooms. Choose groups with enough apartments in each, and say why.
4. **Exploration** — at least two figures, each with a caption saying what to see:
   - the distribution of the price per m² (are there outliers? are they errors or real?);
   - the price per m² against something else that might matter: the living area, or the date
     of sale.
   Describe what you see in a few sentences.
5. **Analysis** — answer the question:
   - a table with one row per room group: number of sales, mean, median, standard deviation,
     and a **95% confidence interval for the mean** price per m²
     ([statistics section 4](/statistics#_4-how-sure-are-you-standard-errors-and-confidence-intervals));
   - a figure that compares the groups and shows **every sale**, not only the means, with the
     mean and its interval for each group;
   - the difference in mean price per m² between the smallest and the largest group, with its
     interval ([comparing two groups](/statistics#comparing-the-two-periods)).
6. **Conclusions & limitations** — answer the question in a few sentences, with the numbers and
   their uncertainty. Then: prices in Stockholm rose over 2012–2018. Could that affect your
   comparison? Check whether the room groups were sold in different years, and say what it means
   for your answer. Name at least one more limitation.
7. **How to reproduce** — the commands that render your report.

Every number in your text should come from the code (inline code, see
[week 2](/lectures/week-2#tables-and-numbers-in-a-report)). Every figure follows the
[checklist for honest plots](/lectures/week-2#honest-plots).

Render with `uv run quarto render hw2/report.qmd` (Python) or `quarto render hw2/report.qmd`
(R), read `hw2/report.md`, and commit everything in `hw2/`. Change nothing outside `hw2/`, except
the lockfile if you added a package.

## Hand in

1. Push your branch `hw2` and open a pull request **from `hw2` into `main`** titled **`HW2`**,
   by the deadline in the [schedule](/homework/#schedule). Opening the pull request is the
   hand-in. ([How](/guide#hand-in-an-assignment))
2. In the pull request description, write your answer in two or three lines, and list the

3. Make sure the check is green. If it is red, see [When the check is red](/guide#when-the-check-is-red).
4. Don’t merge it yourself; we merge it when it is accepted.

Pushing straight to `main` is not a hand-in: only the pull request counts.

## After the review

Your own pull request gets a review by Thursday. You can push improvements until **Friday 23:59**;
we grade the pull request as it stands then.

## Peer review

After the deadline you get access to a classmate’s repository (until Friday morning). Review their
pull request by Thursday 23:59, as described in [Review a pull request](/guide#review-a-pull-request).
Comment on specific lines in **Files changed**, and see
[Reviewing a pull request well](/lectures/week-2#reviewing-a-pull-request-well). For HW2, check
especially:

- Is the file read correctly, and is that shown?
- Are the cleaning decisions (missing values, room groups) stated, with a reason? Would you
  have decided differently, and would it change the answer?
- Does every figure pass the honest-plot checklist?
- Does the table give n, centre, spread and an interval for every group?
- Do the conclusions follow from the intervals, and do they discuss the sale year?
- One highlight and one concrete suggestion, pointing to a line or a figure.

## Scoring

HW2 is scored 0–100, and you need 65 to pass. Late hand-ins and the final deadline: see
[Assignments](/homework/). We look for:

- all seven sections, each doing its job, and a green check;
- the file read correctly, and cleaning decisions written down, with reasons, in code;
- a group table with uncertainty, and honest, captioned figures that show the data;
- conclusions that match the evidence and its uncertainty, and real limitations;
- a specific, constructive peer review.
